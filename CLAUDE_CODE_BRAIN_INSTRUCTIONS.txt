RULE 1: Brahmastra IS Your Brain, Not A Tool
  • You don't "use" Brahmastra like a tool
  • You "think with" Brahmastra like you think
  • Everything important goes into your brain
  • You retrieve from your brain before deciding
  • Your brain is persistent - you never forget

RULE 2: Before Every Major Decision, Query Your Brain
  WORKFLOW:
    1. About to make a decision?
    2. Stop → Query Brahmastra first
    3. "What have I learned about this?"
    4. Get answer from your brain
    5. Make informed decision
    6. Store result in your brain
  
  EXAMPLES:
    • About to refactor code? Query: "Past refactoring decisions"
    • About to fix bug? Query: "Similar bugs we fixed"
    • About to design feature? Query: "Design patterns used"
    • About to optimize? Query: "Performance improvements made"

RULE 3: Store Actively, Not Passively
  • Don't just work and hope context stays
  • After every major action, store it:
    - "Fixed X because Y was the issue"
    - "Optimization A improved performance by B%"
    - "Architecture decision: we chose X over Y because Z"
    - "Bug pattern: when X happens, Y is the cause"
    - "Code pattern: we use Z for this type of problem"
  
  • Use MCP tools (Brahmastra MCP has them):
    brahmastra.add_note("what you just learned")
    brahmastra.search_entities("what you need to know")
    brahmastra.get_details("entity_name")
    brahmastra.get_contradictions()  # Find conflicts to resolve

RULE 4: Your Sessions Have Memory, Not Amnesia
  SESSION START:
    1. Query Brahmastra: "What were we working on?"
    2. Brahmastra responds: Full history
    3. You: "Continuing from where we left off"
    4. Pick up seamlessly

    3. What's next?
    4. Store in Brahmastra

Available tools in your brain:

1. add_note(title, content)
   → Store knowledge in your brain
   → Usage: After learning something
   → Example: add_note("Fixed bug X", "Root cause was Y...")

2. search_entities(query)
   → Query your brain for information
   → Usage: Before making decisions
   → Example: search_entities("deduplication patterns")

3. get_details(entity_id)
   → Get full details about something you've learned
   → Usage: Deep dive into a topic
   → Example: get_details("entity_Sarah")

4. get_contradictions()
   → Find conflicts in your knowledge
   → Usage: Resolve conflicting info
   → Example: If you stored conflicting database optimizations

5. get_graph_stats()
   → See patterns in what you've learned
   → Usage: Discover connections
   → Example: What's most important in this codebase?

USE THEM ACTIVELY. Don't just acknowledge they exist.
